Daily programme

What a day at Little FLOW looks like.

The daily rhythm gives children certainty, while leaving plenty of room for their own pace, spontaneous play and unexpected discoveries. The times are indicative — if something really catches the children’s attention, we don’t end it just because “it’s time to move on”.

7:30 — 8:30
🚪

Arriving and starting the day

Children arrive gradually and have time to settle calmly into the space and the group. They can choose free play, an activity centre, a book or a quiet task of their own. The teacher has room to welcome each child, meet the parent briefly and notice the mood a child arrives with.

8:30 — 9:00
🔵

A shared morning

The community circle helps children understand what lies ahead, share their experiences and sense the group. The morning also includes movement, a short exercise, rhythm or a language activity.

9:00 — 9:30
🍎

Morning snack

Eating together is also an opportunity for independence, conversation and ordinary social habits. Czech and English appear naturally, depending on the situation and the teacher present.

9:30 — 10:15
🔭

The main learning offer

This is where the weekly FLOW rhythm shows most. One day children explore in activity centres, another they create, experiment, work on a project or prepare for an outing. This is not frontal teaching. Children work alone, in pairs or in a small group, and the teacher guides them with questions, materials and support.

10:15 — 11:45
🌳

Outdoors and in the real world

Time outside isn’t just “getting some air”. Children observe nature, move, work with their surroundings, go on outings and connect what they’re exploring indoors with the real world.

11:45 — 12:30
🍲

Lunch and the shift to a calmer part of the day

Lunch together, hygiene and a window for collecting children on the shorter programme. The pace of the day gradually slows.

12:30 — 14:00
🌙

Rest, as each child needs it

Some children sleep, others just need a quiet moment. We offer a story, relaxation, reading or quiet activities. What matters is that a child can recharge in the way that suits them.

14:00 — 15:00
☕

A slow return to the day

Waking, hygiene, stretching, calm play and an afternoon snack. The transition is never rushed.

15:00 — 17:00
🎨

Afternoon FLOW

The afternoon isn’t waiting for parents. Children can return to an unfinished project, create, work in activity centres, be outside or follow their own play. The smaller group gives the teacher room for individual work, observation and support for a particular child.

Why the day isn’t a fixed timetable

The daily rhythm is an indicative frame. When something really catches the children’s attention, we give them time to finish it or take it further.

The shorter day ends at 12:30, the full-day programme at 17:00. See the rates under tuition.

And what changes during the week?

The same daily rhythm is joined by the weekly FLOW rhythm.

Discovering · Creating · Investigating · Experiencing · Moving and sharing.

Experiential learning

A project doesn’t end at the classroom door.

Children learn best when they can truly experience the world. So we head out regularly, meet interesting people and connect projects with real places, situations and experiences.

Nature and ecology

Trips into nature, ecology programmes, gardening, observing the natural world and themes of sustainability. We are gradually building partnerships with ecology centres so children can get to know nature in the field.

Horses and caring for animals

With the Quo Vadis riding centre in Hloubětín, children get to know the world of horses, learn to approach them with respect, notice their needs and gain experience they simply cannot have in a classroom.

Meeting across generations

With the Mezi námi project and the Senevida care home we bring children together with seniors. These meetings make room for talking, reading, making things and other activities where two generations can meet naturally.

Movement and new skills

Where possible we also want to offer swimming, skating and other physical activities that build coordination, independence and the joy of moving.

Stays away from the kindergarten

During the year we also want to offer children bigger shared experiences, such as an outdoor school and, depending on age and group, a winter stay or a ski course.
