What Little FLOW does differently

Six pillars of Little FLOW.

Three pillars of pedagogy plus three pillars of environment and relationships. Together they form a unique kindergarten.

01

Project-based learning in practice

Children explore a topic in depth — through movement, creation, discussion and collaboration. Example: Our city — a walk to the bakery, building a neighbourhood model, mini exhibition for parents.

02

Czech and English every day

A bilingual environment from morning to afternoon. Example: morning circle in Czech, snack in English — always a natural context, never forced.

03

Technology without screens

Bee-Bots, Ozo-Bots and Cubetto — physical programmable toys. Hands-on, tied to projects. No passive content consumption.

04

Max 8 children, 2 adults

A teacher and assistant every day. A small group — every child gets attention. Calm environment, space for all emotions.

05

Smooth transition to FLOW Elementary

Joint events, portfolios, classroom visits. Children know their future teachers and spaces long before starting 1st grade.

06

Partnership with parents

Twigsee app, regular photos and messages from the teacher, child portfolio, community events for the whole family.

Czech and English

Two languages, one childhood.

English at Little FLOW is not a subject or an after-school club. It is the day’s second language — children meet it in situations that make sense.

How it works

Language belongs to the moment

Morning circle in Czech, snack in English, the project block depending on the topic. A child isn’t learning vocabulary — they’re using a language because they need it right then.

No pressure

Understanding comes before speaking

Nobody is pushed to answer in English. Children understand first, then start on their own. A silent period is a normal part of the process, not a problem.

Who it fits

Czech and international families

Czech stays the language a child grows up in, at home and at kindergarten. English is added on top. It works for families who don’t speak Czech at all.

Project example

Our city in three steps.

This is what a project looks like in practice — from a question, through creation, to an exhibition for parents.

Step 1 · Explore

A walk to the bakery

Children get to know their neighbourhood first-hand. Questions come naturally — and with them, the project’s theme.

Step 2 · Create

Building a neighbourhood model

What the children saw becomes a model of the neighbourhood. Movement, creation, discussion and collaboration — exactly what project-based learning means.

Step 3 · Share

Mini exhibition for parents

Children guide their parents through the model and show what they made. The project ends with sharing — and another chapter in the child’s portfolio.

Who is with your child

Two adults for eight children.

Not one tired teacher facing a full class. Every day the group has two adults who know the children by name and can tell what each of them needs right now.

Teacher

Leads the projects and the day

Plans the project block, holds the rituals, follows where each child is moving. Writes to parents about what went well that day.

Assistant

Is there where needed

A second pair of hands and eyes. A child who needs calm or an extra explanation doesn’t have to wait until someone has time.

English

A native speaker in the day

English comes into the day from a person, not a recording. Children hear it in ordinary moments — at snack time, during play, outdoors.

You’ll meet your child’s actual teachers at an Experience Morning — that’s exactly why we run it.
